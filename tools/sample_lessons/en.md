# OBTS · 35 Sample Lessons

## General Bible Studies & Theology (GB)

### 1. How can the Bible help me make a difficult decision?

*Curriculum topic · The doctrine of Scripture (GB-M02)*  
**Read** Psalm 119:105 and 2 Timothy 3:14–17.

**Lesson objective** Learn to turn to Scripture when making a difficult decision.

Sometimes you know you have to decide, but there are good reasons on both sides. Psalm 119 compares God’s word to a lamp for our feet. Picture someone walking in the dark with a lamp. They may not see the whole road, but they can see where to put their foot. As you read the Bible, ask yourself, *What faithful step can I take today?*

Paul reminds Timothy that Scripture teaches and corrects us. A passage may lead you to question why you lean toward one option. Read the verses before and after it. Once you understand the passage’s message, you can see how it relates to your decision.

**Exercise** Write down a decision you face. Name one thing you learned from these passages and the step you will take because of it.

### 2. Where is God when I’m hurting and my prayers seem to go unanswered?

*Curriculum topic · Prayer and spiritual growth (GB-M12)*  
**Read** Psalm 13 and Mark 14:32–36.

**Lesson objective** Learn to tell God honestly what you are going through.

When you have prayed about the same thing for a long time, you may find yourself saying, *God, how long?* That is the question we meet in Psalm 13. David tells God about his sorrow and asks for help. He ends by trusting God’s steadfast love while he is still waiting for an answer.

In the garden of Gethsemane, Jesus also speaks openly about his sorrow. He tells the Father what he wants and entrusts himself to the Father’s will. Start with your own words. Tell God what happened, how it hurts and what help you need today.

**Exercise** Write a three-sentence prayer. First, name what hurts. Second, ask for help. Third, say what you want to entrust to God.

### 3. I made the same mistake again. How do I start over?

*Curriculum topic · The doctrine of sin (GB-M06)*  
**Read** Romans 7:15–25 and 1 John 1:8–9.

**Lesson objective** Understand what repentance looks like in everyday life.

Perhaps you have said, *I won’t do it again*, only to find yourself doing the same thing. In Romans 7, Paul describes wanting to do good and finding himself doing the opposite. As you read, think about what makes it easy to repeat a mistake. What happens just beforehand? What do you tell yourself at that moment?

In 1 John 1, we are reminded that God is faithful to forgive us when we confess our sins. Begin by telling God plainly what you did. Then consider what action would fit your repentance. You may need to apologize to someone, change your circumstances or ask a trusted person for help.

**Exercise** Choose one habit you want to change. Write down a situation in which you tend to fall back into it and what you will do the next time.

### 4. Why does Jesus’ death on the cross matter to me today?

*Curriculum topic · The doctrine of salvation (GB-M08)*  
**Read** Romans 5:6–11 and 2 Corinthians 5:17–21.

**Lesson objective** Explain how Jesus’ death relates to forgiveness and the restoration of our relationship with God.

When you hear that Jesus died for you, you may wonder, *What does that mean for me today?* In Romans 5, Paul says Christ died for us while we were still sinners. God loved us before we could put right what we had broken.

In 2 Corinthians 5, Paul uses the word *reconciliation*. He is talking about God restoring our relationship with him through Christ. As you begin to understand that forgiveness, you can also reconsider how you handle mistakes in your own relationships. You might stop defending yourself, admit where you went wrong and ask for forgiveness.

**Exercise** Imagine a friend asks what it means to be reconciled to God. Answer in two sentences in your own words.

### 5. How can I tell whether the Holy Spirit is guiding me?

*Curriculum topic · The doctrine of the Holy Spirit (GB-M09)*  
**Read** 1 Thessalonians 5:19–22 and Galatians 5:22–23.

**Lesson objective** Learn to weigh something you take to be the Holy Spirit’s guidance.

Sometimes an idea comes to you as you pray. You may feel it strongly and still wonder, *Is God guiding me here?* Paul urges believers to test everything and hold on to what is good. Start by asking whether the step you are considering agrees with what the Bible teaches.

Then think about how the decision would lead you to treat people. Would it show love and patience? Can you exercise self-control when you are angry? Paul names these qualities when he describes the fruit of the Spirit. If the decision has serious consequences, take time to pray and speak with a spiritually mature person who will listen and ask honest questions.

**Exercise** Write down a decision you are considering. Name one biblical teaching that helps you weigh it and one quality of the fruit of the Spirit you want to show as you act.

## Bible Overview — 66 Books (B66)

### 1. What did God ask Adam and Eve after they hid?

*Curriculum topic · The five books of Moses (B66-M02)*  
**Read** Genesis 2:15–17 and 3:6–13.

**Lesson objective** Explain what changed in the relationship between God and human beings after their disobedience.

In the garden, Adam and Eve hid when they realized they had broken God’s command. Follow the conversation and you will see shame give way to fear, then to blame. In the middle of it, God calls to Adam and asks where he is. The question opens the conversation Adam was trying to avoid. Genesis shows us a break in the relationship that the Bible keeps returning to.

**Exercise** Draw a timeline of three events in Genesis 3:6–13. Which event shows most clearly how their disobedience changed the relationship?

### 2. How do you start over in a country you don’t know?

*Curriculum topic · Historical books I (B66-M03)*  
**Read** Ruth 1:14–22 and 2:8–12.

**Lesson objective** Trace how loyalty and generosity emerge in the book of Ruth.

Ruth arrives in Bethlehem after losing her husband, among people she does not know. She chooses to stay with Naomi and later goes to the fields to find food. Boaz notices her, listens and makes sure she has a safe place to glean. As you read, notice how the everyday choices of these three people open a way forward for a family whose story began in grief.

**Exercise** Name one choice Ruth made and one thing Boaz did. Explain how each affected Ruth or Naomi.

### 3. What can I do when I have no words to pray?

*Curriculum topic · Poetry and wisdom literature (B66-M05)*  
**Read** Psalm 42:1–11.

**Lesson objective** Identify how images and repetition carry a psalm’s message.

The writer of Psalm 42 longs for God as a deer longs for water. He remembers worshipping with others. Now he is in tears, and people ask where his God is. The question he asks his own soul comes back more than once. Read the psalm aloud and you can hear both his sorrow and the way he reminds himself he still has reason to hope in God.

**Exercise** Find one poetic image and one recurring line. Explain what each helps you understand about the writer’s situation.

### 4. Why did Jesus stop to hear someone the crowd tried to silence?

*Curriculum topic · The four Gospels (B66-M08)*  
**Read** Mark 10:46–52.

**Lesson objective** Trace how Mark shows faith through the story of Bartimaeus.

Bartimaeus hears Jesus passing and calls out. People rebuke him, but he keeps calling. Jesus stops and asks what Bartimaeus wants him to do. Mark ends by saying that Bartimaeus followed Jesus along the road. Read the story in context and notice how Jesus’ question gives Bartimaeus room to say what he needs.

**Exercise** Compare how the crowd treats Bartimaeus before and after Jesus stops. What changes the way they speak to him?

### 5. Where does the Bible end after beginning in a garden?

*Curriculum topic · Revelation and the unity of the biblical story (B66-M12)*  
**Read** Genesis 2:8–10 and Revelation 21:1–5, 22:1–5.

**Lesson objective** Explain how images from Genesis return at the end of the Bible.

Genesis begins with a garden, a river and the tree of life. At the end of Revelation, the river and tree of life appear again, this time in the image of a city. At the center is the promise that God will live with his people and wipe away their tears. Read the passages together and you begin to see how the hope at the end of the Bible connects with the story’s beginning.

**Exercise** Name two images that appear in both Genesis and Revelation. In one sentence each, explain what hope they help express.

## Pastoral English (PE)

### 1. How can you welcome a church visitor so they feel at home?

*Curriculum topic · Foundations of Pastoral English (PE-M01)*

**Lesson objective** Welcome a visitor and offer help in simple sentences.

A visitor may walk into church unsure where to sit or whom to ask. Begin with a smile and a short sentence like *“We’re glad you’re here.”* Then ask *“Is this your first time with us?”* and give them time to answer. If they need help, say *“I can show you where to sit.”* That last sentence offers something they can act on right away.

**Exercise** Record a 20-second greeting. Introduce yourself, welcome the visitor and show them where to sit.

### 2. How can you read a Bible passage in English so people understand it?

*Curriculum topic · Reading and explaining the Bible in English (PE-M02)*

**Lesson objective** Introduce a Bible passage and explain its main idea in simple English.

Before reading, tell people the book and chapter so they have time to find the passage. Say *“Let’s read Psalm twenty-three, verses one to three.”* When you finish, explain one idea you want them to remember. For example, *“The psalm describes God as a shepherd who cares for his people.”* Read and speak calmly so listeners can follow your point.

**Exercise** Read Psalm 23:1–3 aloud. Introduce it in English and explain one image you find in the passage.

### 3. How can I pray in English when I lose my words in front of people?

*Curriculum topic · English for prayer (PE-M03)*

**Lesson objective** Lead a short prayer in English.

If you are asked to pray without warning, think of one thing the people there need. You could begin, *“Lord, thank you for bringing us together.”* Then make a request that fits their situation, such as *“Give us wisdom as we make this decision.”* Pause briefly between sentences so those listening have time to join you in prayer.

**Exercise** Prepare a three-sentence prayer for a family going through a difficult time. Pray it aloud in about 30 seconds.

### 4. What can you say in English when someone shares their grief with you?

*Curriculum topic · Pastoral conversations and counselling (PE-M06)*

**Lesson objective** Respond with compassion and ask a question that gives the person room to talk.

If someone says *“I lost someone close to me,”* you can reply *“I’m sorry for your loss.”* Give them room to sit in silence or go on speaking. If they seem ready to talk, ask *“Would you like to tell me about them?”* They can decide how much to share. Before you finish, you might ask *“How can we support you this week?”*

**Exercise** Act out a four-line conversation. Use one expression of sympathy and one open-ended question.

### 5. How can you close a sermon in English so people know what to do next?

*Curriculum topic · English for preaching and teaching (PE-M04)*

**Lesson objective** Close a sermon by restating its main idea and giving listeners one practical step.

At the end of a sermon, a listener may remember your story and still wonder where to start. Restate the main teaching in one sentence. Then connect it to something they can do. In a sermon about encouraging others, say *“This week, call one person who may be feeling alone.”* Give listeners a moment to think of someone who comes to mind.

**Exercise** Choose a sermon topic. Write one sentence to restate the teaching and one sentence to describe what listeners can do this week.

## Spoken English (ES)

### 1. How do you introduce yourself to someone new without getting stuck?

*Curriculum topic · Foundations of spoken English (ES-M01)*

**Lesson objective** Introduce yourself and keep the conversation going with one question.

Start with things you already know about yourself. Say *“Hi, I’m Neema. I’m from Mwanza. I’m studying cartography.”* Then ask *“What about you?”* Practise saying each sentence calmly. If you want to add more, listen to the other person’s answer first so you know where to take the conversation.

**Exercise** Record yourself introducing yourself in three sentences and asking one question. Listen to the recording, then try again at a pace that suits you.

### 2. How do you ask for directions somewhere unfamiliar?

*Curriculum topic · Everyday vocabulary and conversation (ES-M04)*

**Lesson objective** Ask for directions and repeat the key part to check that you understood.

Begin with *“Excuse me, how do I get to the bus station?”* If someone replies *“Go straight and turn left after the market,”* repeat the key part: *“Turn left after the market, right?”* Even if you only understood part of the directions, repeating it gives the person a chance to correct you before you leave.

**Exercise** Draw a route from your home to the market. Give someone three steps to follow in English.

### 3. What can you do when someone speaks too quickly for you to follow?

*Curriculum topic · Pronunciation and listening skills (ES-M06)*

**Lesson objective** Ask someone to repeat a message and confirm an important detail.

If you did not hear something clearly on the phone, say *“Could you say that again, please?”* If you need the person to slow down, say *“Could you speak a little more slowly?”* When you hear an important detail, repeat it as a short question such as *“Did you say fifteen or fifty?”* This is especially useful for numbers, times and dates.

**Exercise** Ask someone to say three numbers or three times. Ask them to repeat one, then confirm in English what you heard.

### 4. How do you explain what you can do in a job interview?

*Curriculum topic · English for work and study (ES-M10)*

**Lesson objective** Describe a skill by giving an example of work you have done.

Saying *“I work well with others”* is a start. Give the listener a picture of what you did. You could say *“I helped my team prepare weekly reports. I checked the figures before we sent them.”* These sentences show the task and your contribution. Choose a real example that you can explain if someone asks a follow-up question.

**Exercise** Answer *“Tell me about a skill you use at work or at school.”* Name the skill, where you used it and what you did.

### 5. What do you do when you forget a word halfway through a conversation?

*Curriculum topic · Building fluency and speaking confidence (ES-M07)*

**Lesson objective** Keep a conversation going by describing something when you forget its name.

If you are looking for the word *“umbrella”* and it will not come, say *“It’s something you use when it rains.”* The other person will often understand or supply the word. You can also use *“What I mean is…”* when you need to put an idea another way. Practise describing familiar objects and you will begin to speak more freely even with a small vocabulary.

**Exercise** Describe three household items in English without naming them. Ask someone to guess what they are.

## Christian Leadership (CL)

### 1. What do you do when asked to lead people with more experience than you?

*Curriculum topic · Biblical models of leadership (CL-M02)*  
**Read** Exodus 18:13–24.

**Lesson objective** Recognize how a leader can listen to advice and share responsibility.

Moses spent the entire day hearing people’s disputes. Jethro saw this and told him how the arrangement was wearing out both Moses and those waiting for him. Moses listened. With a new team, start by asking those who were there before you which tasks get held up and why. Then decide what experienced team members can handle without waiting for you.

**Exercise** Think of one task that everyone brings to you. Who could oversee it, and what authority would you give them?

### 2. Who does the work nobody wants to do?

*Curriculum topic · Servant leadership (CL-M05)*  
**Read** John 13:3–17.

**Lesson objective** Use Jesus’ example to spot a need that a leader can address.

When Jesus washes the disciples’ feet, Peter is surprised to see his teacher doing that work. Afterward, Jesus tells them he has given them an example to follow. In your group, someone may arrive early to set out chairs or stay late to clean every week. Ask yourself what help they need and how the work could be shared fairly.

**Exercise** Identify one necessary task that usually falls to the same person. Talk with them about how the team could help.

### 3. How do you tell someone the truth when you’re afraid they’ll get angry?

*Curriculum topic · Conflict management and reconciliation (CL-M09)*  
**Read** Matthew 18:15–17 and Ephesians 4:15.

**Lesson objective** Open a difficult conversation respectfully and listen to the other person’s perspective.

If you enter the conversation having already judged the person, it is easy to miss something you have not understood. Jesus teaches us to speak first with the person concerned. Describe what you observed in simple terms. You could say, “We agreed to send the schedule on Friday. It arrived on Sunday, and the team couldn’t prepare. Can you help me understand what happened?” Then listen before discussing the next step.

**Exercise** Write two sentences to open a conversation about a problem at work. Describe what happened and ask one question that leaves room for a reply.

### 4. Why does your team wait for you even on small decisions?

*Curriculum topic · Team building and delegation (CL-M07)*  
**Read** Acts 6:1–7.

**Lesson objective** Delegate work by making the responsibility, authority and follow-up clear.

In Acts 6, complaints about food distribution brought an unmet need to light. The apostles involved the community in choosing people to take responsibility for it. When you delegate today, explain the result you want, what resources are available and which decisions the person can make independently. Agree on when you will talk again so they can get help early.

**Exercise** Choose a responsibility. Write down the expected result, one decision the person can make and when you will check progress.

### 5. Who can ask you hard questions about ministry funds?

*Curriculum topic · Ethics, authority and accountability (CL-M12)*  
**Read** 2 Corinthians 8:16–21.

**Lesson objective** Set up a simple way to account for how contributions are handled.

When Paul discusses the collection, he explains how they arranged for people to oversee it so the work would be honorable before God and before others. In a small ministry, make it clear who receives the money, who records it and who checks the records. If someone asks what a contribution was used for, the team should be able to answer from records that are easy to read.

**Exercise** Make a sample table with columns for the date, money received, spending and the person who checked the records.

## Entrepreneurship & Business Growth (ENT)

### 1. How can you tell whether your idea solves a problem people actually have?

*Curriculum topic · Finding and testing business opportunities (ENT-M02)*

**Lesson objective** Test a customer problem before spending money to develop a product.

Suppose you want to deliver meals to office workers. Before buying equipment, talk to people who work nearby. Ask where they got lunch yesterday, how long it took and what was inconvenient. If the same problem keeps coming up, try a small service with a few orders. You will see whether people choose it when they face the problem again.

**Exercise** Write three questions about what potential customers do today. Start by asking what they did the last time they faced the problem.

### 2. Why am I selling every day but still have no money left?

*Curriculum topic · Pricing, costs and profitability (ENT-M09)*

**Lesson objective** Distinguish sales revenue from the profit left after expenses.

Suppose you sell 10 cups of juice at 1,000 shillings each. Your sales bring in 10,000 shillings. If the fruit, cups, ice and transport cost 8,000 shillings, you have 2,000 shillings left before other costs such as rent or wages. Writing down your costs early helps you see what price or sales volume the business needs to cover its expenses.

**Exercise** Choose a product. Calculate the revenue from selling five units, subtract the cost of making them and list any costs you have not included.

### 3. Why would a customer buy from you if others sell the same thing?

*Curriculum topic · Business models and customer value (ENT-M03)*

**Lesson objective** Explain a benefit the customer can recognize before buying.

Two sellers may offer the same product. One might have an order ready before the customer leaves work. For someone short on time, knowing the order will be ready when they need it may be enough to choose that seller. Think about whom you serve, what inconvenience they face and what you can promise to deliver consistently.

**Exercise** Complete this sentence for your business. “I help ___ get ___ in a way that saves them ___.”

### 4. How do you respond when a customer criticizes you publicly without making it worse?

*Curriculum topic · Customer service (ENT-M11)*

**Lesson objective** Acknowledge the problem in a complaint and offer a next step.

If a customer posts that an order arrived late, read the whole message before replying. You might say, “Thank you for telling us. We understand you needed your order sooner. Please send us your order number privately so we can see what happened.” Once you have the details, give them a realistic time for your reply. Record the reason for the delay so your team knows what to change.

**Exercise** Write a three-sentence reply to a customer who received the wrong product. Include when they will hear back from you.

### 5. Can your business keep running on a day when you’re away?

*Curriculum topic · Building a business that can operate without the founder (ENT-M20)*

**Lesson objective** Write down one process that someone else can follow.

If every order, discount and complaint needs your attention, your business will stop whenever you are away. Start with one task, such as taking morning orders. Write down the information to collect, where to record it and when the person should call you. Ask someone to follow the instructions for a sample order. Where they get stuck will show you what needs more explanation.

**Exercise** Write four steps for one recurring task. Ask someone to follow them and show you which step needed clarification.

## Using AI for Work, Business and Life (AI)

### 1. When AI sounds confident, how can you tell whether it’s right?

*Curriculum topic · Where AI makes mistakes and how to verify its answers (AI-M01)*

**Lesson objective** Spot a claim that needs checking before you use it.

Imagine AI tells you that registration for a training course closes on Friday. The answer may sound convincing even if the date comes from last year. Take one claim, such as the deadline or a fee, and check it against the organizer’s official announcement. Compare the year, location and conditions. If you cannot verify it, say that the information still needs to be checked.

**Exercise** Ask AI to explain something you know. Pick one claim in its answer and check it against a relevant source.

### 2. Why does AI keep giving me an announcement I can’t use?

*Curriculum topic · Writing instructions that produce usable work (AI-M02)*

**Lesson objective** Write a request that specifies the audience, essential details and the format you need.

If you write “Draft an announcement,” AI will fill in the gaps by guessing. Say who it is for and what you want readers to do afterward. For example, ask for a four-line WhatsApp message for parents who want the training schedule. Give the date and location you have confirmed and tell AI to ask you if any detail is missing.

**Exercise** Write a brief request for an announcement. Rewrite it with the audience, three essential details and one action the reader should take.

### 3. If AI gives you five sources, how do you know which one supports your point?

*Curriculum topic · Research and short reports with sources (AI-M03)*

**Lesson objective** Connect each claim in a report to a source that supports it.

You may receive a list of links that seem to address your topic. Open each source you want to use. Check the author or organization, the date and the section that contains the information you need. Then put the claim next to the source that supports it. If the source covers another place or period, explain the difference before using its figures.

**Exercise** Choose a narrow topic from work or school. Write two claims and find a passage in a source that supports each one.

### 4. You have 30 seconds. How will your video begin so people keep listening?

*Curriculum topic · Video ideas, scripting and storyboarding (AI-M05V)*

**Lesson objective** Plan a short video with an opening question, an example and a final action.

When making a teaching video, begin with something your viewers will recognize. For example, “Have you ever sent an announcement that people read but nobody answered?” After the question, show one example and explain what to improve. Use AI to outline the video, then read your lines aloud to see whether they fit into 30 seconds.

**Exercise** Write a 30-second video script about something you know. Plan a shot or scene for the beginning, the middle and the end.

### 5. When visitors land on your website, will they know where to click without asking you?

*Curriculum topic · Website contact options, publishing and testing (AI-M10)*

**Lesson objective** Test whether a visitor can complete one important task on a website.

You can use AI to draft a button label and the instructions on the page. Then ask someone who has never seen the site to do one task, such as finding where to sign up, on their phone. Watch them try. If they get stuck, ask what they expected to see. Use their answer to improve the button label, its placement or the message that appears after a click.

**Exercise** Write down one action you want a visitor to take on your page. Ask someone to try it and note where they hesitated.
